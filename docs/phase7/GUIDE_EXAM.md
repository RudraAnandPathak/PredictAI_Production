# Exam Intelligence guide

1. **Subject.** Exam tools → choose a project → create a subject (e.g. "Physics, Grade 12").
2. **Documents.** Add documents you already uploaded (syllabus/curriculum, notes, slides, past papers; give each past paper its year). Text comes from the Phase 3 reader. Scanned pages need OCR (Tesseract) and images are only supported inside scanned PDFs. A standard account can keep 3 documents; Special access lifts the limit.
3. **Check the reading.** Questions and curriculum rows are extracted by rules; each has a confidence and warnings. Wrong chapter/topic mapping can be corrected (`/exam/questions/{id}/mapping`); corrections are append-only and are used by the next analysis.
4. **Analyse.** *Analyse papers* produces, per chapter: historical frequency (number of questions), relative emphasis (share of marks), recurrence across papers, last year seen, and a **heuristic score** (weights 40% marks share, 30% recurrence, 20% count share, 10% recency). The page repeats the caveat: this is what happened in the papers you supplied, **not the probability of a future paper**. Chapters never asked, and questions that could not be matched, are listed.
5. **Mock exam.** Choose how many multiple-choice, short and long questions. A blueprint spreads marks across chapters (by the analysis if present, otherwise evenly) and a difficulty mix. Generation is deterministic for a given seed. Each question shows where it came from: *past paper*, *built from your material*, *from a template* (check it) or *edited by you*.
6. **Validate and approve.** Blocking problems (empty paper, duplicates, invalid multiple choice, marks not adding up) must be fixed first; warnings (ambiguous question, missing answer, uncovered chapter, reused past question) must be acknowledged. Approval is explicit and never automatic. Rejected or draft papers are never presented as published.
7. **Report.** Exam analyses can be used as the source of an Exam Intelligence report.
